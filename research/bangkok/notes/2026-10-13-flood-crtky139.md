# Bangkok flood data without Traffy Fondue (CRTKY-139)

Researched: **2026-10-13**, by the open-data research agent. Unblocks the flood
part of **CRTKY-131** (flood dealbreaker) without the Traffy Fondue CSV
registration. All checks below were run by me in this session; commands and
outputs are quoted. Nothing here is an implementation decision — the owner
adopts sources.

**TL;DR:** `thaifloodrisk.com/en/open-data` (CC BY 4.0, Zenodo DOI, GitHub
mirror) ships an exactly-50-row per-khet street-flooding CSV (2021–2026, from
BMA Drainage and Sewerage Dept) with strong district variance, plus a
subdistrict-level GISTDA 2011 flood-extent CSV. Adopt the by-district CSV as
the primary flood signal; keep the 2011 share as a long-memory secondary. The
safety-proxy half of CRTKY-131 is **not** unblocked by any of these sources.
GISTDA's own ArcGIS REST portal works and is usable as a secondary source;
WRI Aqueduct is modelled raster, reject as primary. `data.bangkok.go.th` is
unreachable from this machine (again).

### 1.1 The two Bangkok CSVs we would use

**`/data/bangkok-street-flooding-2021-2026.csv`** — downloaded 2026-10-13
(`curl -sL …/bangkok-street-flooding-2021-2026.csv`). Exactly **50 data rows**,
one per khet. Header:

```
slug,district,district_th,episodes,episodes_2021,…,episodes_2026,days,max_height_cm,median_duration_min
bangkok-phra-nakhon,Phra Nakhon,พระนคร,13,0,2,2,1,4,4,12,30,35
```

Source per the page: **BMA Drainage and Sewerage Department**
(data.bangkok.go.th dataset `frd_dds`; dds.bangkok.go.th) — i.e. this is BMA's
own reported standing-water episodes, republished openly. Updated 2026-10-04;
2026 rebuilt through 2 Oct.

**`/data/bangkok-street-flooding-episodes-2021-2026.csv`** — downloaded
2026-10-13: **1,762 data rows**. Header:
`date, date_end_if_multi_day_report, district_th, district_slug, road_th,
road_normalized_th, location_th, depth_cm, duration_min_verified, rain_mm`.
Sample: `2021-10-02,,เขตดินแดง,bangkok-din-daeng,ประชาสงเคราะห์,…,20,130,57`.
Thai-only text fields (roads, locations) — the district join can use
`district_slug`.

**Consistency check (passed):** district rows sum to 1,762 = episodes row
count (computed with python3/csv over both downloads).

### 1.2 District variance (the deliberability question)

Computed over the 50-row CSV (python3/csv, 2026-10-13):

- episodes per district: min **0**, median **19.5**, p90 **95**, max **150**
  (Ratchathewi); total 1,762.
- Top 6: Ratchathewi 150, Din Daeng 131, Chatuchak 108, Lak Si 107,
  Khlong Toei 103, Watthana 95.
- **22 districts ≥ 30 episodes; only 3 districts = 0** (Nong Chok, Saphan
  Sung, Thawi Watthana) — those 3 have empty `max_height_cm` /
  `median_duration_min` too (Thung Khru has 1 episode, missing duration).

Verdict: the spread is ample for percentile normalisation across 50 districts
— a real flood dealbreaker, not a degenerate signal. Caveats to surface in
honesty notes:

- Counts are **reported episodes on main roads**, 2021–2026 — a
  complaint/observation series, not a hazard model. 2025–2026 dominate
  (2021–2024 counts are much lower per district), so the 5.5-year window is
  one weather regime; fine for relative ranking, state the window in
  methodology.
- Zeros in far-eastern districts (Nong Chok) plausibly mix "no flooding" with
  "no reports"; the GISTDA 2011 layer (below) covers those districts and can
  be blended as the long-memory signal.
- `depth_cm` values look banded (10/20/25/30/40/50 cm — report categories,
  not gauge readings).

## 2. GISTDA flood layers (ArcGIS REST) — secondary, ADOPT (for 2011 signal)

https://gistdaportal.gistda.or.th/data/rest/services/FL_Flood?f=pjson —
fetched 2026-10-13, **no auth**, server 12.1. 19 services:

- `FL_RepeatedFlooding_GISTDA_50k_Y2005_Y2016` (FeatureServer + MapServer):
  layer 0 "พื้นที่น้ำท่วมซ้ำซาก ปี 2005 - 2016", **polygons**, fields
  `year2005…year2016` (12 flag fields), `flood_freq` (SmallInteger),
  `area_m`/`area_rai`, plus tambon/amphoe/province names & codes in Thai and
  English (`tb_en`, `ap_en`, `pv_en`…). District aggregation = group by
  `ap_en` (amphoe = khet).
- `FL_flood_GISTDA_50k_Y2009…Y2014` — six annual 1:50k MapServers
  (confirms the 2009–2014 annual polygons from the ticket).
- `flood_daily*`, `flood_freq11_20`, `flood_freq_central/_northeast`,
  `public_disaster*`, DDPM/RID services — not needed.

Coverage check (run 2026-10-13): count-only query with a Bangkok Web Mercator
envelope (inSR 102100, 11166507,1632847,11237006,1702573) → **{"count":
133311}** polygons intersect the bbox. Bangkok is well covered.

**License: unconfirmed.** Service `copyrightText` = "GISTDA" (layer-level
copyright string empty); no license text is exposed by the REST metadata. The
2011-by-district figures are also available inside the CC BY 4.0
thaifloodrisk CSV (source "GISTDA; OpenGISData-Thailand"), which gives us a
licensed path without depending on GISTDA's own terms.

The thaifloodrisk 2011 CSV (downloaded 2026-10-13): 154 subdistrict rows /
50 unique districts; fields `district_en, district_th,
district_share_flooded_2011_pct, district_share_flooded_2012_2024_pct,
subdistrict_en, subdistrict_th, subdistrict_code, subdistrict_area_km2,
subdistrict_share_flooded_2011_pct, page`. Variance: district share 0–
**93.8 %** (Don Mueang), median 24.4 %, **46/50 districts > 0** — the exact
complement of the street-flooding zeros (Thawi Watthana 90.7 %, Nong Chok
84.4 % in 2011 while reporting ~0 episodes 2021–2026). A second column
`district_share_flooded_2012_2024_pct` (GISTDA satellite) exists but is thin:
only 10 districts > 0, max 22.7 %.

## 3. WRI Aqueduct Floods v2 — REJECT as primary (possible future outlook)

https://datasets.wri.org/datasets/aqueduct-floods-hazard-maps (fetched
2026-10-13): riverine + coastal **inundation depth (m)** under baseline and
2030/2050/2080 projections, multiple return periods, coverage 2010–2080,
version 2.0, released **2020**, and — quote — "The hazard layers have been
simulated without considering the presence of flood protection." License
**CC BY 4.0** per https://www.wri.org/aqueduct ("All Aqueduct products …
Creative Commons Attribution International 4.0 License"; fetched 2026-10-13).
Resolution / exact return periods not confirmed from the fetched pages.

Why reject for the district rating: it is a global **modelled hazard raster**
with no drainage/pumping considered — Bangkok's extensive drainage means it
would systematically overstate present-day flooding, and it carries zero
observed-event signal (the opposite of what a "flood dealbreaker" means to a
resident). Aggregation would also require zonal statistics over global
rasters. Keep as a possible future "future risk" annotation, not a rating
input.

Consequence for CRTKY-131: this research unblocks the **flood dealbreaker**
but **not** the safety proxy (the Traffy Fondue complaint types — lighting,
risk spots — remain the only candidate, still gated on registration).

## 5. Recommendation

- **Primary flood signal:** `thaifloodrisk.com/data/bangkok-street-flooding-2021-2026.csv`
  (50 khets, episodes + days + max depth + median duration, 2021–2026, CC BY
  4.0). Normalise to percentiles like every other category; cite
  "ThaiFloodRisk.com (2026), Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23260465, CC BY 4.0;
  underlying data: BMA Drainage and Sewerage Department".
- **Secondary/long-memory:** GISTDA 2011 district flood share from the same
  collection (`bangkok-2011-flood-by-district.csv`) — covers the
  street-flooding zeros; optionally `gistda_flooded_years` from the
  risk-zones CSV. GISTDA's own REST (§2) stays the raw fallback if we ever
  need the polygons.
- **Reject** WRI Aqueduct as a rating input; keep as outlook material.
- **Safety proxy:** unchanged — no open district-level crime data; Traffy
  Fondue registration (CRTKY-131) remains the only path.
