## Card preview (proposed)
> Phaya Thai is a 9.6 km2 district whose three BTS stations (Ari, Sanam Pao, Saphan Khwai) straddle the Phahonyothin corridor, but the daily experience splits between Ari's low-rise cafe sois, Saphan Khwai's dense commercial blocks, and a Vibhavadi-Pradiphat band of Army and state compounds that holds most of the mapped green. The namesake BTS/ARL station is actually across the boundary in Ratchathewi [phaya-thai-c03, c06, c08, c09].

## Guide lead (proposed)
Phaya Thai khet is best read as three districts in one [phaya-thai-c14]. Along Phahonyothin, Ari's Soi 1-7 grid pairs mid-rise condos with cafe-filled quiet sois, while Saphan Khwai two stops north concentrates shops, apartments and a junction that police close for major events [phaya-thai-c08, c09]. Between them, Pradiphat still carries its military-institutional character, and along Vibhavadi the Army Club and stadium occupy the largest green block - land you can see but not freely use [phaya-thai-c06, c10]. Rents reflect the split: portal asks from the 12,000s in older stock to 35,000+ in Ari's newer buildings [phaya-thai-c11].

## Strengths
- Three BTS stations inside the khet plus the ARL interchange one stop south - the district's core transit promise [phaya-thai-c03] *[`phaya-thai-c03`]*
- Ari's walkable cafe sois one soi off the arterial, with street-food-priced eating on Soi Phahonyothin 7 [phaya-thai-c08] *[`phaya-thai-c08`]*
- Deep everyday healthcare: five private/military hospitals in-district plus Phramongkutklao across the boundary [phaya-thai-c13] *[`phaya-thai-c13`]*
- Wide rent ladder (roughly 12,000-45,000 THB asking) letting budget and mid-market renters live within walking distance of the same line [phaya-thai-c11] *[`phaya-thai-c11`]*

## Trade-offs
- Phahonyothin rush-hour congestion and the Saphan Khwai junction chokepoint, which police advise avoiding city-wide on big-event days [phaya-thai-c09, c12] *[`phaya-thai-c09`, `phaya-thai-c12`]*
- Public green is pocket-scale: BMA's named parks here are expressway-margin leftovers of ~1-2 ha, while the big green is restricted military land [phaya-thai-c06, c07] *[`phaya-thai-c06`, `phaya-thai-c07`]*
- Flood exposure on some low sois in heavy rain, per the district guide's own cons list [phaya-thai-c12] *[`phaya-thai-c12`]*
- Evening quiet: the area is cafe-daytime oriented and 'sleepy' after dark for those wanting nightlife [phaya-thai-c12] *[`phaya-thai-c12`]*
- Identity confusion is structural: Phaya Thai Road, BTS Phaya Thai, Phaya Thai Palace and Victory Monument are all outside the khet they appear to name [phaya-thai-c03, c05] *[`phaya-thai-c03`, `phaya-thai-c05`]*

## Day / night

## Claim index

- `phaya-thai-c01` **[proposed·neutral·entire_unit]** Khet Phaya Thai covers 9.595 km2 and since 26 July 2017 has consisted of exactly two khwaeng: Sam Sen Nai (4.923 km2) and Phaya Thai (4.672 km2); numbers 2-5 of the old khwaeng sequence belong to the area split off to form Ratchathewi district in 1989. Population estimates differ by source and year: 70,238 (2017, EN wiki), 66,212 (Dec 2021, EN wiki), 64,037 (Dec 2023, TH wiki) - a declining trend across published snapshots; the app baseline of ~67,388 (2020) sits within this range. _(scope: district — Khet Phaya Thai (both khwaeng))_
- `phaya-thai-c02` **[proposed·neutral·entire_unit]** The district's boundaries are water, road and rail lines, not neighborhood edges: Khlong Bang Sue to the north (against Chatuchak), the east side of Vibhavadi Rangsit Road to the east (against Din Daeng), the south side of Din Daeng Road plus Khlong Sam Sen to the south (against Din Daeng and Ratchathewi), and the northern railway line to the west (against Dusit). _(scope: district — khet boundary ring)_
- `phaya-thai-c03` **[proposed·neutral·partial]** Three BTS Sukhumvit Line stations lie inside the khet - Ari (N3), Sanam Pao (N4) and Saphan Khwai (N5), all opened 5 December 1999 on Phahonyothin Road - plus SRT Sam Sen railway station; but the khet's namesake station, BTS Phaya Thai (N2) with the Airport Rail Link terminus (A8, opened 23 August 2010), sits just outside the southern boundary in Ratchathewi district. The interchange that gives the district its airport connection is across the line. _(scope: district — BTS Ari/Sanam Pao/Saphan Khwai (inside); BTS+ARL Phaya Thai (outside, southern edge))_
- `phaya-thai-c04` **[proposed·neutral·partial]** Phahonyothin Road is the district's main spine - EN wiki records that it was originally named 'Sanam Pao', after a former firing range, which explains the BTS Sanam Pao station name. Phaya Thai Road, despite the district name, does not pass through the khet at all (it runs through Ratchathewi and Pathum Wan). Pradiphat Road runs 1.808 km westward from Saphan Khwai junction into Dusit; the Vibhavadi Rangsit corridor forms the eastern edge. _(scope: district — Phahonyothin / Pradiphat / Vibhavadi corridors; Phaya Thai Rd (outside))_
- `phaya-thai-c05` **[conflicted·neutral·outside]** Several places marketed under the 'Phaya Thai' name are outside the khet: Phaya Thai Palace stands on Ratchawithi Road by Khlong Sam Sen in Ratchathewi district (13.76833, 100.53278), now the Phramongkutklao Hospital site; Victory Monument and its BTS station are likewise in Ratchathewi (Phaya Thai station sits 'between Victory Monument and Ratchathewi' on the line); Chatuchak's big parks (Chatuchak Park, Vachirabenjatas/Rot Fai Park) lie north across Khlong Bang Sue. _(scope: venue — Phaya Thai Palace; Victory Monument; Chatuchak-side parks)_
- `phaya-thai-c06` **[corroborated·neutral·partial]** The district's dominant green-and-low-rise block is not a park but state/military land: EN wiki describes Royal Thai Army holdings on the district's eastern side 'including Army Stadium and Channel 5 TV', and Thai wiki lists the Army Sports Stadium (สนามกีฬากองทัพบก), the 2nd Cavalry Division, elements of the 1st Infantry Regiment, and the Border Patrol Police headquarters among district features. The Army Club (Vibhavadi) is recorded at 195 Vibhavadi Rangsit Rd, khwaeng Sam Sen Nai, with a full-size football stadium of about 20,000 seats; further military units (1st Artillery Regiment King's Guard, Army Transport, Air Force Engineer Department) line Pradiphat Road. Access to this green is institutionally controlled, not general public parkland. _(scope: corridor — Vibhavadi/Army Club-stadium band and Pradiphat units)_
- `phaya-thai-c07` **[corroborated·tradeoff·entire_unit]** BMA's own Greener Bangkok site lists only small, named pocket green spaces for the khet: Suan Rom Suk (@Phaya Thai), Suan Yan Phahonyothin (@Phaya Thai, mouth of Soi Ruammit), Suan Ari Samphan, Suan Phaya Thai Phirom, and Suan Rama VI (@Phaya Thai, opposite Sam Sen Witthayalai school). The largest single named public park, Suan Phaya Thai Phirom, opened 4 September 2011, is about 10 rai (~1.6 ha) on EXAT land between the Si Rat expressway and Khlong Sam Sen near Phramongkutklao - third-party maps put it at 13.77204, 100.53212 and some label it Ratchathewi, so even the district's flagship park sits at the administrative edge. The app's 14.8 ha of mapped parkland is therefore best explained as the Army Club/Army Stadium compound plus these expressway/leftover-space pocket parks and institutional grounds, not a network of ordinary public parks; the Ari station-unit gap the pilot found is consistent with Suan Ari (the Public Relations Department garden) sitting at the far end of Soi Ari Samphan, beyond an 800 m station radius. _(scope: district — khet-wide park inventory)_
- `phaya-thai-c08` **[corroborated·strength·inside]** Ari is the district's signature subarea: a low-rise cafe-residential quarter off Soi Phahonyothin 7. - `phaya-thai-c14` **[corroborated·neutral·entire_unit]** District-average scores mask four incompatible daily realities inside 9.6 km2: (1) Ari's quiet cafe sois, (2) Saphan Khwai's wholesale/commercial congestion with official traffic-avoidance advisories, (3) the Vibhavadi-Pradiphat band of Army and other state compounds that hold the district's green but restrict access, and (4) the plain apartment/townhouse grid of Sam Sen Nai-Inthamara. - `phaya-thai-c15` **[corroborated·neutral·entire_unit]** Recent administrative and infrastructure history: khet created 1966 from Dusit and Bang Kapi parts and named for Phaya Thai Palace; Ratchathewi split off 1989 and Din Daeng 1993; khwaengs reorganized into two (Sam Sen Nai, Phaya Thai) on 26 July 2017. Transit changes: BTS stations 1999; ARL interchange 2010; the SRT halt at Phaya Thai has drawn criticism for neglect as its tracks are handed to the Don Mueang-Suvarnabhumi-U-Tapao high-speed rail project. - `phaya-thai-c16` **[proposed·neutral·partial]** Vajiravudh College - sometimes assumed to anchor green space here - is in fact in Dusit district (197 Rajvithi Road), on the far side of the railway boundary, so the district's big-campus green candidates reduce to the Army compound, the PRD garden at Soi Ari Samphan, and the expressway-margin pocket parks. Sam Sen railway station (all SRT trains except Eastern Line pass through toward Hua Lamphong) is the district's other rail asset besides the three BTS stations. _(scope: venue — Vajiravudh College (outside, Dusit); Sam Sen SRT station (inside))_
