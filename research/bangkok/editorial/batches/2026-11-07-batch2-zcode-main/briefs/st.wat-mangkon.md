## Card preview (proposed)
> A metro-stop micro-China-town where 30-baht dumplings, a 150-year-old temple and a dragon-belly station share 300 m — and where the nearest public park is a kilometre away in someone else's catchment.

## Guide lead (proposed)
Wat Mangkon is the station area at the heart of Bangkok's Chinatown, opened in 2019 as a Red-and-gold Blue Line stop that straddles two districts — Pom Prap Sattru Phai on one side of Charoen Krung, Samphanthawong on the other. The useful radius is short and clipped: about 450-500 m west and southeast the nearest-stop rule hands streets to Sam Yot and Hua Lamphong, which is why Wat Traimit and the Chinatown Gate already belong to the Hua Lamphong stop's signage while the temple, the Sampeng wholesale lane and the Yaowarat food rows belong here. Days run on commerce and temple hours — herb drinks from 07:00, wholesale porters, a Monday rest day for famous stalls — and evenings on street food and the Soi Nana cocktail lane, which this unit shares at its boundary. What you trade: no documented park inside the catchment, river-tide flood history, fire-prone wooden alleys, and the busiest footpaths in Thailand's densest district.

## Strengths
- Eating density within a 300 m walk of the exits is exceptional even by Bangkok standards — 13 named venues with dishes from 30 to 150 THB and staggered morning-to-midnight hours. *[`wat-mangkon-c05`]*
- The car-free access stack is real: three metro exits with Exit 1 about 180 m from Yaowarat, one stop to Hua Lamphong, a bus stop at street level, and the Ratchawong pier river route — the district's old parking problem is solved by not driving. *[`wat-mangkon-c09`, `wat-mangkon-c10`]*
- Living heritage is at the door: the largest and most important Chinese Buddhist temple in Bangkok sits roughly 200 m from the platform and sets the calendar (Lunar New Year, vegetarian festival). *[`wat-mangkon-c04`, `wat-mangkon-c18`]*
- The unit sits on the receiving end of two heritage-led waves — Song Wat's 2023 'coolest neighbourhood' recognition to the southeast and the decade-long rebirth of the Soi Nana bar lane — though both are partly shared with neighbouring rated areas. *[`wat-mangkon-c07`, `wat-mangkon-c08`]*

## Trade-offs
- Fire safety is the sharpest downside: the 2024 Trok Pho blaze showed fire engines cannot enter these alleys — 63 mostly-tenant households in semi-wooden blocks with two exits — and another fire hit the Khlong Thom block in 2026. *[`wat-mangkon-c13`]*
- Flooding history is river-specific, not abstract: the 2011 high tide put water in these Chinatown streets, receding within hours but proving the low-lying exposure. *[`wat-mangkon-c14`]*
- The food rows that define the unit are under policy pressure: the 2026 citywide vendor crackdown is relocating kerbside sellers to hawker centres, and Yaowarat's 2024 tourist surge brought enforcement and crowd management rather than calm. *[`wat-mangkon-c11`, `wat-mangkon-c12`]*
- Green and quiet are structural deficits here: no public park is documented inside the catchment, and the large parks nearby (Rommaninat, Golden Mount) belong to other rated units or sit 1+ km out. *[`wat-mangkon-c17`]*
- Housing is a thin market: barely 30 condo listings across the two districts, mostly in buildings nearer other stations, so the app's 13,000/26,000 THB figures are district asking blends, not observations of homes you could actually rent by this platform. *[`wat-mangkon-c15`]*

## Day / night
- Morning belongs to the old economy — herbal-drink stands and noodle shops from 07:00, temple prayers and horoscope-fixing, wholesale trade through Sampeng Lane; several famous stalls close Mondays. *[`wat-mangkon-c05`, `wat-mangkon-c04`]*
- Evening flips the streets: Yaowarat's food rows and the Soi Nana bars (roughly 18:00 to midnight) take over, and since the 2024 'Rockstar' surge the evening crowd includes a global fan contingent the BMA is actively managing. *[`wat-mangkon-c07`, `wat-mangkon-c11`]*
- The festival calendar, not the weekend, marks the biggest swings — Lunar New Year and the vegetarian festival compress a year's worth of visitors into the temple's frontage. *[`wat-mangkon-c18`]*

## Within-area variation
- Northwest (Pom Prap half): temple, shrine alleys and the Khlong Thom hardware/electrical block — commerce that serves residents, marginally inside at ~450 m. *[`wat-mangkon-c04`, `wat-mangkon-c19`]*
- Southeast (Samphanthawong half): the Yaowarat retail spine, Sampeng wholesale lane and the Song Wat/Talat Noi fringe, where the cafe-wave and the Hua Lamphong clip meet — the unit's edge is soft here, not a circle. *[`wat-mangkon-c06`, `wat-mangkon-c08`, `wat-mangkon-c03`]*
- The bar lane does not belong wholly to this unit: Soi Nana's Maitrichit end sits on the Wat Mangkon side of the midline, its Rama IV end on the Hua Lamphong side — nightlife attribution should be partial, not binary. *[`wat-mangkon-c07`]*
- Two named anchors often listed with 'Chinatown' — Wat Traimit and the Odean Circle gate — fall outside this catchment on every coordinate check, and Hua Lamphong MRT's own Exit 1 signage serves them. *[`wat-mangkon-c03`, `wat-mangkon-c06`]*

## Conditional fit
- Choose this unit if street food at 30-150 THB, temple rhythms and metro-plus-boat mobility matter more than green space, parking or clinical care within walking distance. *[`wat-mangkon-c05`, `wat-mangkon-c09`, `wat-mangkon-c17`, `wat-mangkon-c19`]*
- Nightlife-oriented residents should aim at the Maitrichit end of the unit, where the bar cluster sits inside the boundary rather than straddling it. *[`wat-mangkon-c07`]*
- Anyone sensitive to crowding, noise or fire-safety economics — or hoping for the app's rent blend to describe a real 1-bed here — should test the trade-offs against the shophouse-heavy stock and 2024-2026 policy churn first. *[`wat-mangkon-c13`, `wat-mangkon-c15`, `wat-mangkon-c12`]*
