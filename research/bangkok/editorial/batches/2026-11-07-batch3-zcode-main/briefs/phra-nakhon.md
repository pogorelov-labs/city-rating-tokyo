## Card preview (proposed)
> Old Bangkok proper: royal parks with posted hours, a river pier on every corner and 171 temples — with Khao San's backpacker strip and a shrinking residential population an honest walk apart.

## Guide lead (proposed)
Phra Nakhon is the heritage core: the Grand Palace, Wat Pho, Sanam Luang and the river piers, with Banglamphu's guesthouse economy and Khao San's nightlife inside the same khet as quiet old shophouse quarters. Its green is genuinely public — Sanam Luang, Saranrom and Santichaiprakan post daily hours — though about half the mapped 38.5 ha is temple and palace grounds rather than open park. Two MRT stations (Sam Yot, Sanam Chai) serve the southern half while Banglamphu relies on boats and buses. Registered population has fallen from 114,124 (1985) to 38,408 (2025), and the trade-offs are tourist monoculture, conservation-constrained housing and no full-service hospital inside the khet.

## Strengths
- Sanam Luang (~11.9 ha, 05:00-22:00), Saranrom (~3.7 ha) and Santichaiprakan (~1.3 ha) are polygon-interior public parks with posted hours — the researched set's most defensible green 10. *[`phra-nakhon-c02`]*
- Fourteen named ferry terminals in-polygon plus two MRT stations give the southern khet genuine river-and-rail access. *[`phra-nakhon-c03`]*
- Cheap for the core: studios and shophouse rooms consistent with the 14,000/28,000 blend, especially in Wang Burapha's shrinking quarters. *[`phra-nakhon-c12`]*

## Trade-offs
- No full-service hospital inside the polygon (Klang is just outside; Siriraj across the river) against an otherwise dense market layer. *[`phra-nakhon-c09`]*
- Tourist monoculture around Khao San and conservation-constrained old housing stock shape what living here is actually like. *[`phra-nakhon-c13`]*

## Day / night
- Ceremonial calendar and tourist high season set the rhythm; Khao San runs late while riverside and temple quarters empty early. *[`phra-nakhon-c10`]*

## Within-area variation
- Banglamphu/Khao San, the royal-field and temple core, and the riverside piers are three different neighborhoods inside 5.5 km². *[`phra-nakhon-c01`]*

## Conditional fit
- Strong fit: heritage-oriented residents and low-budget starters who accept old-building conditions and thin resident services. *[`phra-nakhon-c14`]*
- Poor fit: households needing hospitals, modern condos or quiet tourist-free streets. *[`phra-nakhon-c15`]*

## Explicit unknowns
- green 10 - verified and caveated: The green case is genuinely polygon-interior: Sanam Luang (~11.9 ha, 05:00-22:00, royal-ceremony closures), Saranrom (~3.7 ha, 05:00-21:00), Santichaiprakan (~1.3 ha around Fort Phra Sumen), Rommaninat (former prison, up to ~4.6 ha, in-khet confirmed), plus Mahakan, Nagaraphirom, Utokatan, Maha Chetsadabodin - all with posted daily hours and no admission fee. The honest caveat: OSM-verifiable public green sums to ~19-20 ha, roughly half the app's 38.5 ha; the balance is plausibly temple/palace grounds that are not all publicly usable. Recommend keeping green 10 with a 'public-park subset verified; total includes non-public grounds' note.
- safety 5 - unanchored: No khet-level crime data in either language; the evidence base is a tourist-facing scam/pickpocket genre (oldest body-read source c. 2012) whose own author says violent robberies are uncommon and most visitors will be fine. A mid-low score is plausible via the night-strip vs empty-interior mechanism, but that is inference. Recommend pairing the rating with an explicit 'perception genre, no local statistics' label, as flagged for the sibling sam-yot unit.
- transport 7 - half-coverage rail plus a river network: The district has two underground Blue Line stations inside (Sam Yot, Sanam Chai - the latter with a free underground museum) and Wat Mangkon just outside; the northern half (Banglamphu, Khao San, Samsen) has no rail at all. What carries the rest: 14 named in-polygon ferry terminals, the blue-flag tourist boat terminating at Phra Athit, cross-river ferries, and a weekday-peak-only khlong boat at the north edge. Orange Line (Sanam Luang, Democracy Monument) and Purple Line (Phan Fa) entrance designs were approved 21 May 2025 - future relief, not present. The 7 reads as the midpoint between an unusually good river network and an unusually rail-poor north.
- crowd 2 and the tourist-vs-resident split: The crush is real but venue-local: national arrival volumes (35.5M in 2024, ~32.9M in 2025) funnel into the palace/Wat Pho corridor and the 410-m Khao San strip, while registered residents fell to 38,408 (Dec 2025) and some khwaeng count under 1,800 people. The app's crowd 2 is best explained as venue-adjacency inside an emptying district - and the strip's intensity is being actively managed down by vendor rules, which the food-9 and nightlife-9 stories partially depend on.
- rent 8 inside a conservation cage: Cheap is real at the observed end: HipFlat shows 17 listings, studios from 3,800 THB, 1-beds from ~15,000, residential bulk 13.5-67k - the app's 14,000/28,000 editorial matches. The mechanism worth naming is the conservation regime (height caps, mandated facade colors, committee sign-off, 121 listed buildings): it freezes the skyline and the housing stock, keeps new supply near zero, and leaves the budget tail to old shophouses that portals barely list. Affordability here is inseparable from building age and renovation friction.
- daily essentials 9 - markets yes, hospitals no: The essentials score is carried by a market layer few districts match (Trok Mor, Pak Khlong Talat, Pahurat, Tha Phra Chan, Norrarat, flea and walking-street markets) plus in-khet Big C/Golden Place/7-Eleven coverage. The uncovered flank is acute care: no full-service hospital inside the polygon - Klang just outside on the Pom Prap side, Siriraj across the river, Vajira across the northern khlong. Recommend the guide text keep the market praise and state the hospital gap plainly rather than folding it into a generic 'essentials' sentence.

## Question coverage
- **Q01 — answered**: Khet Phra Nakhon (พระนคร), 5.536 km2, 12 khwaeng (Phra Borom Maha Ratchawang, Wang Burapha Phirom, Wat Ratchabophit, Samran Rat, San Chaopho Suea, Sao Chingcha, Bowon Niwet, Talat Yot, Chana Songkhram, Ban Phan Thom, Bang Khun Phrom, Wat Sam Phraya), bounded by river (W), Khlong Phadung Krung Kasem (N), Ratchadamnoen Nok + Khlong Ong Ang (E). Researched stations: Sam Yot (BL30) and Sanam Chai (BL31) are INSIDE (polygon-verified; batch-3 sibling bundle for Sam Yot); Wat Mangkon (BL29) is OUTSIDE across Charoen Krung in Samphanthawong. Inside: Grand Palace, Wat Pho, Wat Suthat, Giant Swing, Democracy Monument, Khao San Rd, Wang Burapha, Sanam Luang, Saranrom, Santichaiprakan, Rommaninat, all parks in c07. Outside: Wat Arun and Siriraj (across river), MRT Wat Mangkon, Phan Fa Lilat pier (just east of the Ratchadamnoen Nok line), Suan Luang Rama VIII Park (Dusit side despite its riverside position), Princess Mother Memorial Park. App's 2020 population point (44,923) sits between Wikipedia's 2017 (51,231) and Dec-2025 (38,408) registered figures - mid-decline, not contradiction.
- **Q02 — answered**: Three distinct daily lives share the khet. (1) Banglamphu/Khao San (khwaeng Chana Songkhram, Talat Yot, Ban Phan Thom): a guesthouse-vendor economy - hostels at 350-800 THB/night, stalls being pushed into painted boundaries (Apr 2025) and takeaway-only trade zones (Nov 2026), a vendors' association defending street life, and a residential base of only 1,570-5,128 registered people per khwaeng; nights are the strip's, mornings belong to Trok Mor market and locals. (2) Riverside/ceremonial south (Phra Borom Maha Ratchawang, 2,788 residents over 1.647 km2): palace walls, Sanam Luang's 05:00-22:00 regime, Wat Pho, Sanam Chai station with its free underground museum, Saranrom; tourist-heavy by day, ceremonial by calendar, quietest at night. (3) Wang Burapha/inner commercial spine (8,238 residents, the largest khwaeng): shophouse density around Sam Yot station, Big C and Pahurat market, the district's everyday core - with the documented population decline (10,414 to ~8,238-8,344 in Wang Burapha 2019-2025 per batch-2 + Thai wiki) as the structural backdrop. Resident-voice evidence is thin (Reddit/TripAdvisor 403); the split rests on venue/administrative evidence, not interviews.
- **Q03 — answered**: Green 10 - real parks with posted hours (Sanam Luang 11.9 ha 05-22, Saranrom 3.7 ha 05-21, Santichai 1.3 ha, Rommaninat up to 4.6 ha, Mahakan, Nagaraphirom) but the 38.5 ha figure is about double the OSM-verifiable public green (~19-20 ha); mechanism = ceremonial field + palace/temple grounds counted. Food 9 / nightlife 9 / vibe 10 - 1,446 food venues and 147 nightlife venues (app) in 5.5 km2 with Bib Gourmand presence; mechanism = tourist market density layered on resident markets (Pak Khlong, Pahurat, Tha Phra Chan, Trok Mor). Crowd 2 - 35.5M national arrivals (2024) funneled into three walkable venues inside a district of 38,408 residents. Rent 8 (cheap) - thin condo supply under height caps (17 HipFlat listings; 1-bed ~15k) plus old shophouse stock. Transport 7 - two Blue Line stations serve the south/centre only; 14 in-polygon piers + boats carry the rest; the north has no rail. Safety 5 - no unit-specific evidence found in either language (see Q12).
- **Q04 — answered**: Against the postcard: the registered population has fallen from 114,124 (1985) to 38,408 (Dec 2025) - the district is depopulating while visitor infrastructure grows; conservation rules (height caps, mandated facade colors, committee sign-off, 121 listed buildings) protect the fabric but cap housing renewal and supply; the vendor crackdown (no-new-vendor policy, takeaway-only zones) is actively thinning the street-food economy that feeds the food-9 story, with displaced sellers reporting 40-50% income loss; and the flood-2011 record shows the island's riverfront (Tha Phra Chan amulet market, piers) flooded while the interior held behind sandbags - with Banglamphu's namesake tree dying as a documented casualty. None of this contradicts the ratings outright; each is the cost side of the same mechanism.
- **Q05 — answered**: Annual frame: Songkran 13-15 Apr (Khao San water fights; 2024 'Maha Songkran World Water Festival' on Sanam Luang), Royal Ploughing (May), royal kathin, Loy Krathong at the riverbank piers and Santichaiprakan/Phra Sumen, and Ratchadamnoen light decorations for 28 Jul / 12 Aug / 5 Dec. Rolling frame: Sanam Luang's event calendar (Buddha relics Feb-Mar 2024; Beijing tooth relic 5 Dec 2024-14 Feb 2025; marathon finishes) can close the field to general use. Tourist frame: Nov-Feb high season over a 2025 backdrop of 32.9M national arrivals (down from 35.5M). Weekend nights belong to the Khao San/Rambuttri strip under a midnight-closing rule the trade is petitioning (Oct 2026) to relax; weekday peak hours activate the khlong boat at the north edge only.
- **Q06 — answered**: There IS metro inside the khet - a correction to the research lead: Sam Yot and Sanam Chai are polygon-inside (Wat Mangkon is not). But coverage is half the district: no rail for Banglamphu/Khao San/Talat Yot/Samsen, which is the defensible core of transport 7. River access is the compensating network: 14 named in-polygon ferry terminals (app says 24; OSM count lower), blue-flag tourist boat terminating at Phra Athit, express boats and cross-river ferries, khlong boat at Thewes (weekday-peak only). Sidewalk conditions are a live policy variable, not a fixed trait: the BMA's Nov 2026 policy prioritizes footpaths for pedestrians, repaints vendor zones as takeaway-only in 15 zones including Phra Nakhon, and Apr 2025 rules confined Khao San/Rambuttri/Kraisi stalls behind painted lines. No source read quantifies sidewalk continuity or walking comfort for the residential interior.
- **Q07 — answered**: Essentials are market-led: Big C (Charoen Krung/Sam Yot), Golden Place (Tha Chang), 7-Elevens, co-op store; Trok Mor morning market, Pak Khlong Talat, Pahurat/Little India, Tha Phra Chan market + amulets, Norrarat, Lang Wat Ratchanatda flea market, Ong Ang walking street; community libraries incl. Banglamphu and three Thammasat libraries. Healthcare is the structural gap behind daily_essentials 9: no full-service hospital inside the polygon - Klang Hospital sits just outside (Pom Prap side), Siriraj is across the river, Vajira is in Dusit, Police General Hospital is in Pathum Wan (name association with Ratchadamnoen is misleading - verified); in-khet provision is the Red Cross Health Station 2. For a khet whose identity is the nation's ceremonial centre, hospital access requires a river or boundary crossing.
- **Q08 — answered**: App editorial 14,000/28,000 THB matches observable condo asks (HipFlat 1-bed from 15-16k; residential bulk 13.5-67k; studio at 3,800 shows the floor; 17 listings only). The affordability story is really about stock, not price per unit: conservation zoning (height caps 5-7 stories in the conservation zone per academic snippet, 16 m island limit, committee sign-off, facade mandates) means new supply barely exists, the cheap tail is old shophouses and rooms above shops that portals do not list, and renovation is gated - hence rent 8 (cheap) coexisting with visible building decay and a shrinking resident base (Wang Burapha down from 10,414 to ~8.2-8.3k between 2019 and 2025 per batch-2 + Thai wiki).
- **Q09 — answered**: Verified one by one: Sanam Luang - 74 rai 63 sq wah (~11.94 ha; OSM computes 10.99), Royal Household-owned/BMA-run, ancient monument, daily 05:00-22:00 with the central road 24 h, assemblies/vending/sleeping banned, periodically surrendered to crematorium and state-event builds. Saranrom - 23 rai (~3.7 ha), public park since 1960, daily 05:00-21:00, across Ratchini Rd from the Grand Palace. Santichaiprakan - 8 rai (~1.3 ha) around Fort Phra Sumen, opened 5 Dec 1999, daily 05:00-21:00, Loy Krathong venue. Rommaninat - former 1890 prison, ~29 rai (~4.6 ha per snippet; OSM maps 1.05 ha of open green inside walls), 05:00-21:00, gym/basketball + Corrections Museum; in-khet confirmed (batch-2 correction stands). Supporting: Mahakan Fort Park (~1.05 ha), Maha Chetsadabodin (0.41), Utokatan (0.30), Nagaraphirom (0.19). Honest caveats: Siwalai garden is inside palace walls (not public); the 38.5 ha app figure is not reproducible from public parks alone; Sanam Luang's public hours exclude event periods.
- **Q10 — answered**: Strong fit: heritage-first residents who want the island's calendar (ceremonies, river, parks) and accept old-building conditions; low-budget arrivals served by the strip's 3,800-16,000 THB end and market food; night-economy workers and guests for whom Khao San/Rambuttri is the point. Weak fit: quiet-seekers sensitive to the strip's regulated-but-loud nights (midnight closing is a live political fight, Oct 2026 petition); anyone prioritizing in-district hospital access (none inside); households needing modern housing stock (height caps + committee sign-off cap supply); residents dependent on late-evening transit from the northern khwaeng (no rail, khlong boat weekday-peak only). The event trade-off cuts both ways: Sanam Luang's ceremonies close the field exactly when the district is most on show.
- **Q11 — answered**: Documented: (1) Sanam Luang event cycle - Buddha relics (Feb-Mar 2024), Songkran festival + marathon (2024), Beijing tooth relic (Dec 2024-Feb 2025); (2) 21 May 2025 Rattanakosin committee - 121 valuable buildings list with tax incentives, +2.00 m bridge raising, entrance designs approved for Orange Line (Sanam Luang, Democracy Monument stations) and Purple Line (to Phan Fa); (3) vendor regulation tightening - Apr 2025 painted boundaries on Khao San/Rambuttri/Kraisi, Nov 2026 no-new-vendor policy + 15 strict trade zones including Phra Nakhon; (4) nightlife-hours politics - Oct 2026 petition to PM Anutin and a proposed Khao San/Rambuttri 'sandbox' pilot; (5) demographic - registered population 38,408 by Dec 2025, extending the decline. Tourism backdrop softened: 32.9-33.0M national arrivals 2025 vs 35.5M in 2024.
- **Q12 — answered**: Safety 5: no district-level crime statistics found in EN or TH; evidence is a petty-crime/scam genre (Travelfish, c.2012 + 2025-26 guides) with the counterpoint that violent crime is uncommon; treat as perception/unanchored and label low-confidence - the same conclusion the sibling sam-yot bundle reached. Transport 7: mechanism is half-coverage rail (2 stations inside, both southern) + 14 OSM-verified piers + boats; the app's 24-pier count is not reproduced and may count both banks/nodes. Green 10: 38.5 ha not reproducible from mapped public parks (~19-20 ha); gap plausibly temple/palace grounds - an app-methodology question, not a research error. Rent 8: portal sample thin (17 listings; some mis-mapped), cheap tail unmeasured. Also unresolved: the app polygon puts Phan Fa Lilat pier (13.7555, 100.5064) outside the khet while Democracy Monument (13.7586, 100.5021) is inside - consistent with the Ratchadamnoen Nok boundary but worth a central polygon audit; batch-2's PIP note on the monument conflicts with this batch's result. Rommaninat's 4.6 ha rests on a snippet (no body read).

## Claim index

- `phra-nakhon-c01` **[corroborated·neutral·entire_unit]** Phra Nakhon is one of Bangkok's 50 khet, area 5.536 km2 (Thai Wikipedia: 5.536 ตร.กม.), the central district containing Rattanakosin Island and the city pillar shrine (Lak Mueang). It is divided into 12 khwaeng: Phra Borom Maha Ratchawang (1.647 km2), Wang Burapha Phirom (0.720), Wat Ratchabophit (0.220), Samran Rat (0.230), San Chaopho Suea (0.144), Sao Chingcha (0.153), Bowon Niwet (0.496), Talat Yot (0.193), Chana Songkhram (0.339), Ban Phan Thom (0.414), Bang Khun Phrom (0.458), Wat Sam Phraya (0.522). Boundaries (Thai Wikipedia): north - Khlong Phadung Krung Kasem toward Dusit; east - Ratchadamnoen Nok road and Khlong Rop Krung (Ong Ang) toward Pom Prap Sattru Phai and Samphanthawong; south and west - the Chao Phraya midline toward Khlong San, Thon Buri, Bangkok Yai, Bangkok Noi, Bang Phlat. The district office is at 78 Soi Samsen 3, khwaeng Wat Sam Phraya. History: inner-district overhaul of 15 Oct 1915 (Rattanakosin 2458) created the predecessor amphoe including Chana Songkhram and Samran Rat; on 12 March 1928 (2471) several were merged as Amphoe Phra Nakhon; elevated to Khet Phra Nakhon in 1972.
