## Card preview (proposed)
> A working two-line interchange at Bangkok's most ordinary big junction: BTS and MRT share the five-way Lat Phrao crossing via a 300 m skywalk into a 1982 mega-mall, street food starts at the soi mouths, rooms rent from a few thousand baht — and the famous parks are one train stop away, not at the door.

## Guide lead (proposed)
Ha Yaek Lat Phrao is what central-adjacent Bangkok looks like when it is built for the people who live there: two rail lines meeting over a five-way intersection, a 1982 shopping complex bolted to the stations by a walkway, sois of walk-up apartments renting from a few thousand baht, and a small 24-hour park with exercise bars where the green score says '10' mostly on borrowed parks one MRT stop north-west. Traffic runs the ground floor of daily life here; the skywalk runs the second.

## Strengths
- A true dual-network interchange: BTS Sukhumvit (N9, 2019) and MRT Blue (BL14, 2004) at one junction, 300-434 m apart, joined by an elevated walkway into the malls — roughly 8.4 million combined passengers in the 2021 snapshot. *[`ha-yaek-c01`, `ha-yaek-c02`, `ha-yaek-c03`]*
- Central Ladprao puts a 1982-vintage full-service mall (295 stores, department store, 10-screen cinema, hotel, convention hall) directly on the station skywalk — essentials, rain cover and air-con without crossing traffic. *[`ha-yaek-c04`]*
- Street-level rent value is real: 104 live listings near the BTS station, mostly 4,000-10,000 THB walk-up rooms, with 1BR condos from ~14,000 THB — below the app's district blend. *[`ha-yaek-c11`, `ha-yaek-c12`]*
- Cheap, dense everyday food: 50-70 THB stall plates, the Soi Lat Phrao 1 market 209 m from the MRT exit, mall food courts, and a small cafe scene at the junction. *[`ha-yaek-c13`]*

## Trade-offs
- The junction is a chronic congestion node — ~337,000 vehicles/day on the corridor roads, hard peaks twice a weekday, and signal failures that stall all five approaches. *[`ha-yaek-c07`]*
- Green 10 is borrowed: the 30-60 ha parks sit ~1.0-1.8 km away around Mo Chit (one MRT stop); the unit's own green is a small 24h exercise park. *[`ha-yaek-c08`, `ha-yaek-c09`]*
- Nightlife and international dining are thin at the junction; the documented bar cluster is ~2 km east in Lat Phrao district. *[`ha-yaek-c14`]*
- The area is proudly unpolished — Thai-first daily life, spread-out sois, main roads hostile to walking; exactly why vibe scores lowest. *[`ha-yaek-c15`, `ha-yaek-c16`]*

## Day / night
- Morning: trains from ~06:00, stall breakfasts at the soi mouths, coffee at junction cafes from 08:00; Somdet Ya Park runs 24h for early walkers. *[`ha-yaek-c17`, `ha-yaek-c13`, `ha-yaek-c08`]*
- Day: the skywalk corridor between stations, malls and the park loop carries most movement without street crossings. *[`ha-yaek-c01`, `ha-yaek-c16`]*
- Evening: after-work stall dinners (packing up ~21:00 corridor-wide), mall hours to 22:00; night owls ride east to the Nak Niwat/Soi 71 bars. *[`ha-yaek-c17`, `ha-yaek-c14`]*
- Weekend: family malls, then the 200k-visitor Chatuchak Weekend Market one MRT stop away — the district's crowd drains there, not to the junction. *[`ha-yaek-c10`, `ha-yaek-c17`]*

## Within-area variation
- Junction core (stations, skywalk, Central Ladprao, Union Mall): dense, loud, most convenient; everything rated lives here. *[`ha-yaek-c01`, `ha-yaek-c04`]*
- Soi Lat Phrao 1 strip (209-650 m): the quiet local layer — market, walk-up apartments, a slow-bar cafe; the cheapest beds in the unit. *[`ha-yaek-c13`, `ha-yaek-c11`]*
- Somdet Ya Park corner (adjacent to Central Ladprao): the unit's only real green sitting room, 24h, exercise bars, skywalk onward toward Rot Fai Park. *[`ha-yaek-c08`]*
- North along Phahonyothin toward Phahonyothin 24 (~1 km) and the Central Phaholyothin site: construction zone for the 2026 megamall; outside the merge scope but shaping the unit's future crowding. *[`ha-yaek-c06`]*

## Conditional fit
- Pick it if you are a budget renter or family who wants space, family-townhouse living and two train lines, and can live with traffic noise — room rents from ~4,000 THB, townhouses ~18,000-40,000. *[`ha-yaek-c11`, `ha-yaek-c12`, `ha-yaek-c01`]*
- Pick it if your week is mall-and-street-food shaped and you read Thai or are happy pointing at menus. *[`ha-yaek-c13`, `ha-yaek-c15`]*
- Skip it if you want nightlife at the door (bars are ~2 km east), a park out the front (big parks are a train stop away), or tourist-zone polish and English-first services. *[`ha-yaek-c14`, `ha-yaek-c09`, `ha-yaek-c15`]*
- Factor in 2029-2033: the anchor mall will be under a 4-year, 4.5bn-THB renovation from the new lease; expect hoardings in the core. *[`ha-yaek-c05`]*

## Question coverage
- **Q01 — answered**: A two-station interchange node at Bangkok's five-way Lat Phrao junction in Chatuchak district. BTS Ha Yaek Lat Phrao (N9, Sukhumvit Line, opened 9 Aug 2019) and MRT Phahon Yothin (BL14, Blue Line, opened 3 Jul 2004) serve the same junction, measured 300-434 m apart, linked by a ~300 m elevated walkway into Central Ladprao and Union Mall — comfortably within the app's <=450 m merge rule, so 'one scored area' is defensible. Inside the unit: both stations, the skywalk, Central Ladprao + Centara Grand hotel block, Union Mall, the Soi Lat Phrao 1 market strip and Somdet Ya Park 84. Outside: the Chatuchak/Queen Sirikit/Rot Fai park complex and Chatuchak Weekend Market (~1.0-2.1 km WSW around Mo Chit), and the Lat Phrao-district corridor (Nak Niwat/Soi 71, Chokchai 4) to the east. The district assignment 'Chatuchak 100%' is plausible for the station-Voronoi core; land south of Lat Phrao Rd technically belongs to Lat Phrao district and polygon clipping could swap edges.
- **Q02 — answered**: Commuter-rail-centric and street-food-centric, with traffic as the constant backdrop. Mornings run on stall breakfasts and trains; workday peaks jam the five-way junction (~337,000 vehicles/day on the corridor roads); after work the malls and stall rows take over; weekends are family mall trips and the market one stop away. The skywalk lets residents swap lines, shop, and reach the park loop without fighting street level, but away from the cluster the sois are long and the main roads are loud and hard to cross.
- **Q03 — answered**: Transport 8 rewards a genuine dual-line interchange (two networks, ~8.4M combined 2021 passengers, island-platform BTS station) with weather-proof transfers. Daily essentials 8 rewards Central Ladprao (295 stores, department store, Tops Food Hall, cinema, hotel, convention centre) plus Union Mall and a Lotus's-scale hypermarket at the exits. Rent 8 rewards genuinely low street-level asking rents (4,000-10,000 THB rooms; 1BR condos 14,000-28,000). Food 8 rewards density of cheap stalls, the Soi Lat Phrao 1 market and mall food courts. Green 10, however, is mostly rewarding parks that sit 1.0-1.8 km outside the catchment (see Q09).
- **Q04 — answered**: Three complications: (1) Green 10 leans on parks outside the 800 m radius per source coordinates — the unit's own park is a small 24h exercise park; the app's 38.1-ha polygon matches no published park area. (2) Crowd 3 is well supported — this is one of Bangkok's chronic congestion nodes. (3) The area is deliberately unpolished: limited nightlife and international dining at the junction, Thai-first daily life, spread-out sois. Rent 8 may also understate how cheap the room-stock floor is (from ~2,500-4,000 THB).
- **Q05 — answered**: Weekday shape: trains from ~06:00, stall breakfasts, a hard mid-morning and evening congestion peak at the junction, after-work stall dinners (vendors packing up ~21:00 in the corridor), then local bars further east for night owls. Weekend shape: malls 10:00-22:00, family park trips, and the 200k-visitor Chatuchak Weekend Market one MRT stop away pulling crowds off-site. Somdet Ya Park runs 24h. Nothing suggests a late-night economy at the junction itself.
- **Q06 — answered**: Two-tier. The station-mall cluster is genuinely walkable and weather-proof: ~300 m skywalk BTS-MRT-Central Ladprao-Union Mall, Somdet Ya Park loop, Soi Lat Phrao 1 market 209 m from the MRT exit. Beyond the cluster, walking degrades: heavy multi-lane roads with long signals, spread-out sois ('some side sois mean long walks'), and main-road walking described as 'a chore' at rush hour. No radius-to-time conversions are offered; distances are: Central Ladprao adjacent to the skywalk, Union Mall across Lat Phrao Rd via walkway, MRT Phahon Yothin ~300-434 m from the BTS station.
- **Q07 — answered**: Groceries: Tops Food Hall in Central Ladprao, a Lotus's-scale hypermarket at the junction exits, 7-Elevens dense in the sois (corridor guides). Services: the MRT exit list puts Bangkok Employment Office 1 and Saint John's University on the station's Phahonyothin side, and Horwang School at Exit 3. Hospitals: no major hospital inside the unit — Lat Phrao General Hospital is out at Soi 95 and Paolo at Chokchai 4 (corridor guides); treat hospital access as a corridor trip. Fresh markets: Soi Lat Phrao 1 market 209 m from the MRT.
- **Q08 — answered**: The station evidence is cheaper than the 16,000/33,000 THB district blend. Near-station rooms in walk-up buildings ask ~4,000-10,000 THB (104 live listings; examples 4,200-9,500 at 190-650 m; cheapest 2,500-3,800), family townhouses ~18,000-40,000 (corridor), and 1BR condos ~14,000-28,000 (BKK Oracle) or 18,000-30,000 (Home & Travel). The app blend reads as a condo-weighted Chatuchak-district figure; for this unit's actual stock the honest statement is: rooms very cheap, condos mid-range, premium scarce (top listing 42,000). Rent 8 (higher = more affordable) is directionally right and arguably conservative for non-condo stock.
- **Q09 — answered**: Not on its own footprint. Inside the unit the green offer is Somdet Ya Park 84 — a small free 24h park with an exercise-bar terrace and running loop, plus tree cover on the skywalk approaches (area unpublished). The three big parks (30.4 + 22 + 60 ha) that plausibly drive green 10 lie ~1.0-1.8 km WSW around Mo Chit per source coordinates — outside the 800 m radius and, per the working description, north of the MRT Kamphaeng Phet clip; they are one MRT stop away, and BMA says a skywalk connects the junction park toward Rot Fai Park. If the app's 38.1-ha polygon is a mapped park polygon, it matches none of the published areas exactly and its inside/outside status must be settled by the coordinator's polygon check. Quiet, likewise, is not a unit-scale quality: the junction is loud.
- **Q10 — answered**: Good fit: budget renters and families who want space and family-townhouse living at room rents from ~4,000 THB; commuters who need both BTS and MRT; anyone whose life is mall-and-street-food shaped and who reads Thai or is happy pointing. Poor fit: nightlife-first residents (the documented bar cluster is ~2 km east; junction nightlife is thin), walkability-first or park-at-door residents (big parks are a train stop away), and anyone wanting tourist-zone polish or English-first services. Rainy-season note: the corridor's flood exposure is flagged by guides (east sub-area).
- **Q11 — answered**: (1) Rail: BTS arrived 9 Aug 2019 (first northern-extension section, 1.4 km from Mo Chit), then 4 Dec 2019, 5 Jun 2020, 16 Dec 2020 completed the line to Khu Khot — the station is a 2019 arrival, not 2020. (3) Competition/intensification: The Central Phaholyothin (21bn THB, GBA 457,409 m2) completes Q4 2026 about 1 km north, and CPN promotes the corridor as Bangkok's 'next CBD' with land at 1.9M THB/sq.wah. Net: transit arrived, land values and crowding pressure are rising, and the core mall will be scaffolded in the early 2030s.
