## Card preview (proposed)
> Bangkok's smallest district runs on two clocks - wholesale lanes that wind down by mid-morning and a food street that hits full volume around 9pm - and both share the same narrow sidewalks.

## Guide lead (proposed)
Samphanthawong is the 1.42 km2 khet that contains the core of Bangkok's Chinatown: Yaowarat Road, the Sampeng wholesale lane, the Song Wat riverside strip and three khwaeng of shophouses that have traded since the 1782 Sampheng settlement. It is the city's smallest and least populous district yet among its densest, and the app's #1 rank rides on that commerce - hundreds of food venues, boat piers, three MRT exits and 130-plus gold shops packed inside walking distance. Living here is a different proposition from eating here: housing is mostly shophouse floors above working shops, the nearest real park is across the boundary in Phra Nakhon, and the district's defining street turns, by its own guide's account, uncomfortable at midday and loudest after dark. Rent data is thin - a 16-listing asking-rent sample puts older in-khet 1-beds around 12,000-17,000 THB - consistent with the app's 14,000 estimate but far from statistical proof.

## Strengths
- Errands and meals are walkable to an unusual degree: the MRT operator lists Yaowarat, Phadung Dao, the Old Market and the Itsaranuphap market lane as Exit 1-2 destinations, and the orange-flag commuter boats leave from Ratchawong pier at the foot of the district. *[`samphanthawong-c04`, `samphanthawong-c06`]*
- The food economy is a working system, not just a dinner strip - morning dim sum and market trade upstairs-and-downstairs in the same lanes that wholesale into the night street. *[`samphanthawong-c07`, `samphanthawong-c10`]*
- Older in-khet stock rents cheap for central Bangkok: verified asking prices of 12,000-17,000 THB for studios and 1-beds in Talat Noi condos line up with the app's 14,000 estimate. *[`samphanthawong-c16`]*

## Trade-offs
- There is effectively no park inside the khet; the nearest sizeable green space, 4.5-hectare Rommaninat, sits across the boundary in Phra Nakhon near Sam Yot MRT. *[`samphanthawong-c18`]*
- Residential life rides on precarious ground: the century-old Charoen Chai community was already facing monthly-lease eviction pressure in 2016 as metro-driven land values rose, and the district's heritage protection covered just 26 buildings by that count. *[`samphanthawong-c13`]*
- The river edge, not the market core, is the flood-exposed part - in 2011 tide-driven water reached Chinatown and receded within hours while the commercial drag stayed largely dry. *[`samphanthawong-c14`]*
- The street-food economy that anchors the district's scores is under active citywide policy pressure: a May 2026 vendor crackdown is relocating kerbside sellers in packed commercial districts. *[`samphanthawong-c17`]*
- Even after the 2024 peony-tile sidewalk rebuild, residents were complaining about uneven pavements and hazards from street furniture, vendors and motorbikes. *[`samphanthawong-c11`]*

## Day / night
- Before 9am the Sampeng wholesale lane is described as workable only for restocking merchants and early dim-sum buyers; by mid-afternoon vendors are icing seafood on Yaowarat; around 21:00 the main road is 'at full volume' and it thins from about 22:30. *[`samphanthawong-c07`]*
- Festival nights rewrite the schedule entirely: ahead of Lunar New Year 2025 the road was packed enough that visitors warned each other to carry water, and the festival ran nightly to 9 February. *[`samphanthawong-c15`]*

## Within-area variation
- Song Wat is the district's quieter riverside face - spice wholesalers and family shops trading beside the cafes and galleries that joined a Made in Song Wat collective that grew from 10 businesses in 2022 to over 60 - while Yaowarat's frontage carries the noise and crowds. *[`samphanthawong-c12`, `samphanthawong-c07`]*
- Parts of the 'Chinatown' experience are not in the khet at all: the Soi Nana bar strip sits in Pom Prap Sattru Phai, the temple the MRT station is named for lies just outside the boundary, and the priciest nearby 'Chinatown' condo rental found (30,000 THB) is across the line too. *[`samphanthawong-c03`, `samphanthawong-c04`, `samphanthawong-c16`]*

## Conditional fit
- Fits people whose week runs on foot - food-trade workers, market-dependent households, anyone who wants a cheap in-khet room above working commerce with boats and two MRT stations at the edges. *[`samphanthawong-c04`, `samphanthawong-c06`, `samphanthawong-c08`, `samphanthawong-c16`]*
- Poor fit for households that need parkland, quiet evenings near the main road, a car, or a large modern flat - the stock is shophouse-dominated, the green space is across the canal, and the loudest hours are exactly when the district earns its food reputation. *[`samphanthawong-c07`, `samphanthawong-c08`, `samphanthawong-c18`]*

## Claim index

- `samphanthawong-c01` **[proposed·neutral·entire_unit]** Samphanthawong is one of Bangkok's 50 khet and the smallest district by area in Thailand (about 1.42 km2), divided into three khwaeng (Chakkrawat, Samphanthawong, Talat Noi, with the district seat in Talat Noi), and held a registered population of 19,546 in December 2023 at roughly 13,800 people/km2 - the city's smallest headcount on some of its densest ground. _(scope: district — Khet Samphanthawong)_
- `samphanthawong-c02` **[corroborated·neutral·entire_unit]** The district's Chinese community dates to the founding of Bangkok in 1782, when traders were moved from near the Grand Palace to the Sampheng riverbank, and Sampheng Lane (now Wanit 1) remained the main street until Yaowarat Road was cut through from 1892, its winding 'dragon' shape chosen to spare existing homes. _(scope: district — Sampheng / Yaowarat)_
- `samphanthawong-c03` **[proposed·neutral·partial]** The 'Chinatown' label is wider than the khet: Wat Mangkon Kamalawat temple lies just outside the boundary in Pom Prap Sattru Phai, Khlong Thom market sits across Charoen Krung in Pom Prap, River City is in Bang Rak, and the Soi Nana bar alley (between Maitrichit and Rama IV roads, developed as a bar street in the 2010s) is administratively in Pom Prap Sattru Phai even though it is marketed as Chinatown. _(scope: other — 'Chinatown' neighborhood label vs khet boundary)_
- `samphanthawong-c04` **[corroborated·strength·partial]** MRT Wat Mangkon (BL29) opened 29 July 2019 on Charoen Krung Road at Plaeng Nam intersection and physically straddles the Samphanthawong/Pom Prap boundary; its operator lists Exit 1 for Yaowarat/Phadung Dao/Old Market/I'm Chinatown, Exit 2 for Itsaranuphap (Leng Buai Ia market) and Mangkon Road, and Exit 3 for Wat Mangkon Kamalawat and the Poh Teck Tung Foundation. _(scope: venue — MRT Wat Mangkon station)_
- `samphanthawong-c05` **[proposed·tradeoff·outside]** Hua Lamphong station, adjacent in Pathum Wan district, stopped being the long-distance rail terminus on 19 January 2023 when 52 long-distance trains moved to Krung Thep Aphiwat (Bang Sue); it keeps ordinary/commuter and all Eastern-line services, and a 2026 plan positions it as a future SRT Dark Red Line stop. _(scope: venue — Hua Lamphong railway station)_
- `samphanthawong-c06` **[proposed·strength·partial]** The khet's river edge is served by Chao Phraya express-boat piers - Ratchawong described as the gateway to Chinatown, River City, and Si Phraya for Charoen Krung - on the orange-flag commuter route running roughly 06:00-19:00 about every 30-40 minutes at a flat 14 baht, a workhorse service that crowds at rush hour. _(scope: corridor — Chao Phraya riverfront piers (Ratchawong, River City, Si Phraya))_
- `samphanthawong-c07` **[proposed·mixed·inside]** The area runs on two clocks: the wholesale/marketing lanes (Sampeng/Wanit 1) are described as only navigable before about 9am when merchants restock and dim-sum shops sell out by mid-morning, while Yaowarat's street-food rows are set up from late afternoon, hit 'full volume' around 21:00 and thin out from about 22:30 - a rhythm a guide documents hour by hour. _(scope: corridor — Yaowarat Rd / Sampeng Ln (Soi Wanit 1))_
- `samphanthawong-c08` **[proposed·neutral·entire_unit]** Housing in the community is dominated by two-storey-plus shophouses and half-timber buildings with shopfronts below and living/storage space above; land is used mainly commercially, and residents are described as working-age Thai-Chinese plus migrant workers from Isan and inner Bangkok. _(scope: district — Yaowarat/Samphanthawong community)_
- `samphanthawong-c09` **[proposed·tradeoff·entire_unit]** Community documentation records the area's traditions thinning: old cinemas have shrunk from seven to one, home moon-worship has lessened, and no new temples or shrines are being built - existing ones are only preserved and restored. _(scope: district — Yaowarat community)_
- `samphanthawong-c10` **[proposed·strength·inside]** Yaowarat remains a working commercial district: over 130 gold shops line the road, making it the country's largest gold-trading district (turnover cited at roughly ten million baht a day), alongside daytime wholesale and retail markets selling dry goods and Chinese groceries. _(scope: corridor — Yaowarat Rd goldshops and market lanes)_
- `samphanthawong-c11` **[proposed·mixed·inside]** In 2024 the BMA re-laid the district's busiest sidewalks with dark-red peony-pattern non-slip tiles (replacing a 'dragon-scale' design), widening and strengthening them with disabled access considered - in the context of resident complaints about uneven pavements and hazards from street furniture, vendors and motorbikes on 'notoriously perilous' Bangkok footpaths. _(scope: corridor — Yaowarat sidewalks)_
