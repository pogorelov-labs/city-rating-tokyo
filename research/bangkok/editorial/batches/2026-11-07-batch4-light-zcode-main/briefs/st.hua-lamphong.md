## Card preview (proposed)
> A heritage-rail quarter where a 1916 dome fronts an MRT station, daily life runs on Chinatown-edge food and commuter trains, and the loudest thing is the station's own history.

## Guide lead (proposed)
Hua Lamphong pairs the city's grandest rail terminal with a Blue Line MRT stop and a catchment that spills into Chinatown's edge; since long-distance trains left for Krung Thep Aphiwat in January 2023 it has been quieter, event-driven and more residential in feel.

## Strengths
- Layered transport: MRT Blue, remaining commuter and Eastern-line trains, plus canal piers at the station exits. *[`hua-lamphong-c01`, `hua-lamphong-c04`, `hua-lamphong-c07`]*
- Deep, cheap food density pulled from the Samphanthawong share of the catchment, with Wat Traimit at the MRT's own exit. *[`hua-lamphong-c08`]*

## Trade-offs
- The intercity role is gone (52 long-distance trains moved in January 2023), so daily-rail utility is now commuter-scale and the area reads calmer than its reputation. *[`hua-lamphong-c04`, `hua-lamphong-c10`]*
- The green (7) and safety (6) scores rest on inputs this pass could not ground — an unidentifiable 3.5 ha of park and an explicitly editorial safety figure. *[`hua-lamphong-c11`, `hua-lamphong-c12`]*

## Question coverage
- **Q01 — answered**: A dual identity: MRT Blue Line station (BL28, 2004) bolted to the 1916 Italian Neo-Renaissance rail terminal, catchment straddling Pathum Wan, Bang Rak, Samphanthawong and Pom Prap Sattru Phai.
- **Q02 — answered**: Station forecourt, canal edge and the Wat Traimit-side corridor dominate daily experience; food density is the practical anchor of the area.
- **Q03 — answered**: High scores ride on rail connectivity (MRT + commuter + Eastern line), heritage volume and Chinatown-edge food spill; rent 4 reflects premium Pathum Wan blending rather than the adjacent Rong Muang stock.
- **Q04 — answered**: Vibe 10 sits against a post-2023 reality of fewer trains and a visibly reduced station role; green 7 rests on an unidentifiable 3.5 ha; the 2-bed rent is above the closest listing mean.
- **Q05 — answered**: Weekday commuter pulses plus event/weekend heritage traffic; quiet side sois otherwise — a markedly lower daily crowd than the pre-2023 terminus era.
- **Q06 — answered**: Flat grid streets around the station and canal, four MRT exits with named walkways; no walking-duration figures are derived here (radius-derived times are out of scope).
- **Q07 — answered**: Commuter rail for workdays, dense cheap food, a free railway museum and canal pier as the distinctive daily-life furniture; nightlife count is modest and Chinatown-weighted.
- **Q08 — answered**: 1-bed 28,500 is consistent with adjacent-khwaeng listing averages (~29.6-30.2k); the 62,500 2-bed exceeds the closest 2-bed mean (~43.2-43.6k) and needs the premium Pathum Wan caveat.
- **Q09 — answered**: Green 7 cannot be grounded: no 3.5-ha public park was identifiable in sources; quiet is real but is a function of the post-2023 service reduction rather than of parks.
- **Q10 — answered**: Strong fit for heritage-minded commuters wanting MRT access below Silom pricing; weak fit for anyone prioritising parkland or a late-night street scene of its own.
- **Q11 — answered**: Post-2024 markers found: 58 daily trains (June 2024), 108th-anniversary events, free Thai Railway Museum visited May 2025, electric canal-boat promotion reported through Feb 2026 (unattributed); no completed redevelopment decision found.
- **Q12 — answered**: Park identity, safety basis, the 2-bed rent blend, the 58-vs-62 train count, and the SRT development plan's current status are all unresolved; anchors verified clean.

## Claim index

- `hua-lamphong-c01` **[corroborated·neutral·entire_unit]** The unit anchors on MRT Blue Line station BL28 'Hua Lamphong', opened 3 July 2004 under Rama IV Road in front of the 1916 Bangkok (Hua Lamphong) railway station, with an underground pathway linking the two; it was the Blue Line terminus until the 29 July 2019 extension to Lak Song and is now a through-station. App anchor equals the OSM node exactly (13.73757, 100.51713). _(scope: station_area — Hua Lamphong MRT station / Bangkok (Hua Lamphong) railway station)_
- `hua-lamphong-c02` **[proposed·neutral·entire_unit]** App polygons split the catchment across four khet: Pathum Wan 47.4%, Bang Rak 28.9%, Samphanthawong 18.4% and Pom Prap Sattru Phai 5.3%; the station sits at the Pathum Wan side (khwaeng Rong Mueang per batch-3 pathum-wan work). _(scope: station_area — Hua Lamphong catchment (Voronoi ∩ 800 m))_
- `hua-lamphong-c03` **[corroborated·strength·inside]** The rail terminal building opened 25 June 1916 (construction from 1910), designed by Mario Tamagno and Annibale Rigotti in Italian Neo-Renaissance style with an Italian-style dome and stained glass, with 14 platforms; on 19 January 2023 it was officially renamed 'Bangkok (Hua Lamphong) railway station'. _(scope: venue — Bangkok (Hua Lamphong) railway station)_
- `hua-lamphong-c04` **[corroborated·neutral·inside]** On 19 January 2023 all 52 long-distance trains (14 Northern, 18 Northeastern, 20 Southern) were moved to terminate at Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal; Hua Lamphong retains ordinary, commuter, excursion and all Eastern-line services — Thai Wikipedia counts 62 such trains as of 2023-01-19 (Northern 16, Northeastern 6, Eastern 22, Southern 4) while Thai Train Guide reported 58 daily in June 2024. _(scope: venue — Bangkok (Hua Lamphong) railway station)_
- `hua-lamphong-c05` **[corroborated·strength·inside]** Since the long-distance move the station's afterlife is event- and heritage-led: the SRT governor publicly pledged in December 2021 that there would be 'no demolition', the station regularly hosts events (Hualamphong in Your Eyes, Unfolding Bangkok projection mapping, a Cartier brand event), historic locomotives are displayed on platforms 4-5, and the station marked its 108th anniversary in 2024. _(scope: venue — Bangkok (Hua Lamphong) railway station)_
- `hua-lamphong-c06` **[corroborated·strength·inside]** A small Thai Railway Museum operates free of charge in the front (western) wing of the station — open Wednesday-Sunday per a May 2025 visitor review — displaying old tickets, maps, royal railway objects and a simulated carriage interior; a Nation Thailand social post describes it as easy to miss. _(scope: venue — Thai Railway Museum, Bangkok (Hua Lamphong) railway station)_
- `hua-lamphong-c07` **[corroborated·neutral·inside]** The unit's '2 piers' app fact most plausibly refers to Khlong Phadung Krung Kasem canal piers rather than river piers: MRT Exit 2 lists a 'Khlong Phadung Krung Kasem pier' and Exit 4 opens to the canal, while the Chao Phraya riverfront lies well beyond the catchment. _(scope: station_area — Khlong Phadung Krung Kasem (station-adjacent stretch))_
- `hua-lamphong-c08` **[corroborated·strength·partial]** The unit's food weight (app: 531 food, 65 cafes, 39 nightlife venues) is plausibly a Chinatown-edge spillover effect: the catchment includes 18.4% of Samphanthawong khet, and the MRT Exit 1 landmarks — Wat Traimit and Odeon Circle, the Chinatown gateway — sit roughly 600 m from the station by coordinates. _(scope: corridor — Wat Traimit / Odeon Circle edge of Yaowarat)_
- `hua-lamphong-c09` **[corroborated·neutral·partial]** Rent check against stated figures (28,500 / 62,500 THB, Pathum Wan editorial blend): PropertyScout's Rong Muang listing averages are 1-bed ~29.6-30.2k (range 20-40k) and 2-bed ~43.2-43.6k (range 27-55k) across 137 listings — the app's 1-bed figure is consistent, while its 2-bed figure sits well above PropertyScout's 2-bed mean, implying a premium-tower component in the Pathum Wan blend. _(scope: district — Rong Muang (Pathum Wan) residential corridors)_
- `hua-lamphong-c10` **[corroborated·neutral·inside]** Rhythm and crowd: the station historically carried 60,000+ passengers a day (2001) and 'many tens of thousands' (2018); after the 2023 long-distance move the weekday baseline is commuter-and-ordinary trains plus event weekends, so a low crowd score (app: 2) is plausible for daily life even with tourist and event peaks. _(scope: station_area — Hua Lamphong station forecourt and Rama IV frontage)_
- `hua-lamphong-c11` **[corroborated·tradeoff·entire_unit]** The green score (7) rests on an app figure of 3.5 ha of park inside the catchment, but no public park of that size could be identified in accessible sources within ~800 m of the station; the nearest known named parks (Rommaninat, Saranrom) sit roughly 1-2 km away, and the canal-side walk is the closest greenish space found in sources. _(scope: station_area — Hua Lamphong catchment)_
- `hua-lamphong-c12` **[proposed·neutral·entire_unit]** Safety 6 is an editorial input (app confidence: 'editorial', source label 'ai_research'); no accessible crime statistics or systematic safety reporting specific to the station area was found in English or Thai, so the score could not be corroborated or challenged with data in this pass. _(scope: station_area — Hua Lamphong catchment)_
