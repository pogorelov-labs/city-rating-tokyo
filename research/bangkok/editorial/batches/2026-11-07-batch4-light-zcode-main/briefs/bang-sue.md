## Card preview (proposed)
> Bangkok's biggest rail bet is planted here - six stops inside the khet and the ASEAN-scale terminal on its northern edge - but in 2025 the district around it is still rail yards, interchange malls and 13.6k-THB one-beds.

## Guide lead (proposed)
Bang Sue is where Bangkok put its rail future: the Krung Thep Aphiwat terminal (across the khet's northern boundary), the Blue/Purple interchange at Tao Poon, MRT Bang Pho, Purple Wong Sawang and Bang Son, plus the SRT junction - six-plus stops inside 11.5 km2. The present is less grand: Gateway-at-Bang-Sue and Big C carry the essentials, the green is pocket-scale, and 1,054 listed condos rent from about 10k for studios. A 14-year 'smart city' plan (announced November 2025) and the MRT Purple extension (2030 target) are the district's next chapters; until they land, Bang Sue is a commuter's khet with construction in its hair.

## Strengths
- Unmatched rail supply for the price: Blue, Purple, SRT Red lines and the long-distance terminal all interchange on or beside the khet, OSM-verified at six in-khet stations. *[`bang-sue-c04`, `bang-sue-c02`]*
- Deep rental market at low price points: 1,054 portal listings with ~USD389 (1BR) / ~USD598 (2BR) averages in Oct 2026 - the batch's biggest inventory. *[`bang-sue-c07`]*

## Trade-offs
- The hub is ahead of the district: design capacity 624,000/day against a legacy-60k comparator and development only at EIA stage in late 2025 - amenity scores of 6 are structural, not an oversight. *[`bang-sue-c06`, `bang-sue-c05`]*
- Park share 0.6% (8.3 ha) in pocket parks; the promised smart-city green exists on drawings, not on the ground. *[`bang-sue-c09`]*

## Question coverage
- **Q01 — answered**: Khet Bang Sue, 11.545 km2, TWO khwaeng (Bang Sue 5.762 / Wong Sawang 5.783; Tao Poon is a ย่าน, not a khwaeng). In: MRT Bang Pho, Tao Poon interchange, Purple Bang Son/Wong Sawang, Red Bang Son, SRT Bang Sue Junction (all OSM-verified inside). The terminal itself straddles the Chatuchak border (EN wiki; batch-3 concurs); MRT Blue 'Bang Sue' station node unresolved in OSM read.
- **Q02 — answered**: Subarea lives: Bang Pho riverfront (Vietnamese-heritage quarter, MRT), Tao Poon interchange zone (Gateway mall, terminal walkway), Wong Sawang/Prachachuen (Big C, Lotus, Market Place, KMUTNB students). Rail-yard edges between them untextured in sources read.
- **Q03 — answered**: Transport 9: six-plus rail stops inside the khet (Blue Bang Pho, Blue/Purple Tao Poon, Purple Bang Son/Wong Sawang, Red Bang Son, SRT Junction) + the terminal interchange across the boundary + Purple south under construction to 2030. Highest rail density in the batch.
- **Q04 — answered**: Counter-view: the hub has not produced an amenity district - capacity-vs-usage gap (624k/day design vs legacy 60k comparator), 2023 near-empty coverage (snippet-flagged), pilot development only at EIA stage in Nov 2025. DE/food/vibe/nightlife 6s are structurally explicable, not data errors.
- **Q05 — answered**: Rhythm: commuter tidal flows through Bang Pho/Tao Poon interchanges; construction cadence (Purple works, SRTA phases) increasingly sets the calendar; no festival/market rhythm body-read.
- **Q06 — answered**: Walkability prose absent; interchange walkway (terminal-to-MRT underground link) is the one documented pedestrian route (EN wiki KTW article). Rail yards and arterials likely interrupt walking - inference, flagged.
- **Q07 — answered**: Daily life: Gateway at Bang Sue + Big C/Lotus/Market Place for essentials; Mani Phiman second-hand market; KMUTNB student economy; 1,054 marketed condo rentals.
- **Q08 — answered**: Rent 9 / 12,000-25,000: HipFlat 1BR ~USD389 (~13.6k), 2BR ~USD598 (~21k), large interchange condo inventory - editorial band consistent, low end plausible.
- **Q09 — answered**: Green 7 vs 0.6% park share is the batch's weakest green case: pocket parks only (OSM); promised smart-city landscaping unbuilt. Recommend re-scoring input review by coordinator.
- **Q10 — answered**: Fits: budget commuters wanting maximum rail choice, condo investors riding the hub build-out, KMUTNB affiliates. Poor fit: park-seekers, nightlife-first movers, anyone allergic to construction cycles.
- **Q11 — answered**: 2024-2026: SRTA Nov-2025 master plan (14-year smart city, E1/G9 plots, UR Japan deal expected early 2026); Purple south works 67.26% to a Nov-2030 target; land auctions from Feb 2026 (snippet-flagged).
- **Q12 — answered**: Outside-the-score: terminal footprint split across khets unresolved at khwaeng level; MRT Bang Sue station node outside khet area in OSM read (contradicts TH wiki list); platform count 24-vs-26 discrepancy; KTW daily ridership unmeasured in bodies read.

## Claim index

- `bang-sue-c01` **[corroborated·neutral·entire_unit]** Khet Bang Sue (บางซื่อ, geocode 1082) has 11.545 km2 and exactly TWO khwaeng: Bang Sue 5.762 km2 and Wong Sawang 5.783 km2 (Wong Sawang carved out of Bang Sue khwaeng on 20 Jul 2009, effective 21 Sep, along the southern railway line). 'Tao Poon' is a business neighborhood (ย่าน), not a khwaeng - a common misconception this read corrects. Registered population 118,634 (Dec 2023, TH wiki; app 122,410 is a different vintage; EN wiki's 2017 figure 125,440). First an amphoe in 1894, folded into Dusit in 1938, khwaeng under Dusit from 1972, khet on 9 Nov 1989. OSM: khet relation 1639285, khwaeng relations 11507762-63. _(scope: district — Bang Sue)_
- `bang-sue-c02` **[proposed·neutral·partial]** Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal (formerly Bang Sue Grand Station; name given by King Vajiralongkorn) - the largest railway station in Southeast Asia with 26 platforms over ~600 m, 274,192 m2 of usable floor on a 2,325-rai site, cost ~15bn baht - 'located along the border of Chatuchak district and Bang Sue district', infobox address 336 Thoet Damri Road, Chatuchak. Red lines opened 2 Aug 2021; 52 long-distance trains moved from Hua Lamphong on 19 Jan 2023. This corroborates batch-3's straddle finding: the terminal is a boundary object, not an unqualified in-khet landmark. _(scope: building — Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal)_
- `bang-sue-c03` **[corroborated·neutral·partial]** Station-scale corroboration and count discrepancy: Thai Train Guide gives 24 platforms, 274,192 sqm usable area and a 624,000 passengers/day design capacity, and notes that at its writing 'only Red Line commuter trains use the building'; it benchmarks Hua Lamphong's legacy traffic at ~60,000 passengers/day. English Wikipedia counts 26 platforms. The 24-vs-26 difference is unresolved (likely platform vs track counting) - flag whenever the hub is described. _(scope: building — Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal)_
- `bang-sue-c04` **[proposed·neutral·partial]** Which stations sit in the khet: MRT Blue Line Bang Pho (บางโพ) and Purple/Blue interchange Tao Poon (เตาปูน) plus Purple Bang Son and Wong Sawang and the SRT Red-line Bang Son and Bang Sue Junction (ชุมทางบางซื่อ) nodes are all inside khet area 3601639285 in OSM (Thai Wikipedia concurs for each). The MRT Blue 'Bang Sue' station node did NOT return inside the khet area in the OSM read despite TH Wikipedia listing it among the khet's stations - its khwaeng/khet placement is unresolved (it serves the terminal interchange across the boundary). Six rail stops inside the khet support transport 9. _(scope: district — Bang Sue)_
- `bang-sue-c05` **[corroborated·mixed·entire_unit]** The amenity-gap tension (DE 6, food 6, vibe 6, nightlife 6 - the batch's lowest set) has a structural reading: the khet does have retail anchors - Gateway at Bang Sue mall at Tao Poon, Big C Wong Sawang, Lotus Prachachuen, Market Place Wong Sawang, the Mani Phiman second-hand market, KMUTNB campus and Bang Pho Hospital (TH Wikipedia) - but large tracts are SRT rail land only now entering the development pipeline (Thai Post: pilot designs/EIA commissioned in 2025). The hub-adjacent streetscape is transitionary: new rail capacity, old rail edges. Nightlife-41 is the batch's smallest count, consistent with a dormitory-plus-station profile rather than an evening district. _(scope: district — Bang Sue)_
- `bang-sue-c06` **[corroborated·mixed·partial]** The hub exists before the city around it: against a 624,000/day design capacity the comparator is Hua Lamphong's legacy ~60,000/day, and 2023 opening coverage described near-empty halls (AP: 'lone passenger' framing - body not retrievable, snippet-flagged; Thailand Trains guide similarly noted the station 'feels empty'). SRTA's response is a 14-year build-out: 'เมืองอัจฉริยะบางซื่อ' livable smart city / new CBD, plan พ.ศ. 2569-2582, partnership with Japan's Urban Renaissance agency targeting 'the largest rail center in the country and ASEAN', contract signing expected early 2026, phase 1 renting plot E1 for a new Ministry of Transport HQ and plot G9 for private joint investment. _(scope: district — Bang Sue around the terminal)_
- `bang-sue-c07` **[proposed·neutral·entire_unit]** Rent corroboration (rent 9, editorial 12,000/25,000): HipFlat shows 1,054 condo listings - the largest marketed inventory in this batch - with portal averages USD 297 (studio) / 389 (1BR) / 598 (2BR), USD 13/sqm (Oct 2026; ~10.4k/13.6k/21k THB at ~35). Interchange-branded projects dominate: Ideo Mobi Bangsue Grand Interchange, The Tree Interchange, Metro Sky Prachachuen, Chewathai Residence Bang Pho, Chapter One Flow Bangpo ('a peaceful riverside-inspired residence...' on Pracharat Sai 1 with access to MRT Bang Pho, Tao Poon and the terminal), Modiz Collection Bangpho (Dec 2022). _(scope: district — Bang Sue)_
- `bang-sue-c08` **[corroborated·neutral·entire_unit]** Identity texture: the khet's riverfront quarter Bang Pho was an 18th-century Vietnamese (Annamese) immigrant settlement around Wat Bang Pho Omawat; the name 'บางซื่อ' appears in Sunthorn Phu's 1807 journey poem; the Rama VI Bridge (first bridge over the Chao Phraya, opened 1923, rebuilt 1953) links the khet across the river; KMUTNB (King Mongkut's University of Technology North Bangkok) campus and SCG headquarters sit on Pracharat roads. Vibe 6 and crowd 4 read as 'institutional dormitory + rail corridor', not heritage quarter. _(scope: district — Bang Sue)_
- `bang-sue-c09` **[corroborated·tradeoff·entire_unit]** Green honesty (green 7, app 8.3 ha / 0.6%): OSM reads inside the khet show only pocket-scale green - Suan Klong Bang Sue (canal pocket park) and a school-adjacent pocket park (Yothin Burana) - no destination park. The khet's green promise is mostly on drawings (SRTA smart-city landscaping, not built). A 7 against a 0.6% share should be read as canal-and-institution-grounds credit, not parkland. _(scope: district — Bang Sue)_
- `bang-sue-c10` **[corroborated·neutral·entire_unit]** 2024-2026 recent-change spine: (1) SRTA master plan unveiled Nov 2025 with the 14-year smart-city program and 151m-THB design/EIA commission (Thai Post); (2) MRT Purple southern extension - Tao Poon is its in-khet northern terminus - shows civil works 67.26% complete with a November 2030 opening target (MRTA official); (3) earlier reporting (search snippets, not body-read) described Feb-2026 auctions of SRT land plots A/B worth ~100bn THB. The district's direction is set by these projects, not by organic retail growth. _(scope: district — Bang Sue)_
