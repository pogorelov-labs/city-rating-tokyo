## Guide lead (proposed)
Kamphaeng Phet (MRT BL12, opened 2004) is the market's own station: Exit 1-2 drop into Chatuchak Weekend Market's 35 acres, Exit 3 into Or Kor Tor's graded, air-conditioned produce halls, and bus bays with roughly a dozen routes sit at the exits — a transport-10 built on adjacency and buses rather than rail variety, since the BTS interchange is one stop north at Chatuchak Park. Rents track the Chatuchak blend (16,000-25,000 baht studios and small one-beds captured district-wide). The honest caveats: the 0.1 ha of mapped park means green 9 is buffer effect from parks centred in other units, the unit's nightlife and safety scores have no found evidence either way, and the market itself is mid-redevelopment after City Hall's 2025 clock-tower evictions.

## Strengths
- Unmatched market access for a rail stop: direct exits into the weekend market and the daily Or Tor Kor market, plus roughly a dozen bus routes at two on-street stops. *[`kamphaeng-phet-c02`, `kamphaeng-phet-c05`, `kamphaeng-phet-c06`]*
- MetroMall underground and a compact station footprint keep errands indoor-adjacent in rain or heat, at the district's mid-market rent level. *[`kamphaeng-phet-c01`, `kamphaeng-phet-c08`]*

## Trade-offs
- MRT-only: no BTS interchange at the station (one stop away at BL13), ridership one-sixth of the neighbouring interchange, and the transport 10 depends on market/bus adjacency — confirm the rubric. *[`kamphaeng-phet-c03`, `kamphaeng-phet-c09`]*
- Green 9 is borrowed scenery: 0.1 ha mapped in-unit and no park centroid within 850 m; the park complex's centroids lie 1.1-1.5 km away in the mo-chit/chatuchak units. *[`kamphaeng-phet-c04`]*

## Day / night

## Claim index

- `kamphaeng-phet-c01` **[corroborated·neutral·inside]** Kamphaeng Phet is an underground MRT Blue Line station, code BL12, opened 3 July 2004 beneath Kamphaeng Phet Road in Chatuchak district (13.797693 N, 100.548361 E per the article; OSM node 13.79795, 100.54788), between Chatuchak Park (BL13) and Bang Sue, with 1,164,679 passengers in 2021 — the lowest of the three Chatuchak-area rail stops. An underground mall (MetroMall) opened at the station in early 2009. The road is named for Prince of Kamphaeng Phet, the first Thai commander of the State Railway; the station shares its name with a province, and with an SRT Dark Red Line station (RN02) about 3 km north on Kamphaeng Phet 2 Road. _(scope: station_area — Kamphaeng Phet MRT)_
- `kamphaeng-phet-c02` **[proposed·strength·inside]** The operator's own station page defines the unit's front doors: Exit 1 — Chatuchak Weekend Market and the Mo Chit transport connection; Exit 2 — the market, Kamphaeng Phet 2 Road, Bang Sue Junction; Exit 3 — Or Tor Kor (A.T.K.) market, Kamphaeng Phet 1 Road, the Dairy Promotion Organisation, Wat Phai Tan. Buses stop at exits 1 and 3 (routes 49, 96, 134, 136, 138, 145, 509, 536 on the DD Mall side; 77, 96, 134, 136, 145, 509, 517, A1 across from it), with motorcycle-taxi stands at all three exits and a lift at Exit 2. _(scope: station_area — Kamphaeng Phet MRT exits)_
- `kamphaeng-phet-c03` **[corroborated·mixed·entire_unit]** Transport 10 — the plausible mechanism is market-and-bus adjacency rather than rail redundancy: this is an MRT-only stop with no BTS interchange of its own (the BTS transfer is one stop away at Chatuchak Park BL13), yet it has direct underground-market access, two bus clusters with roughly a dozen routes including the A1 corridor service, and the Or Tor Kor daily market at Exit 3. Nothing read measures station-level connectivity quality; the 10 should be read as the app's rubric applied to this asset set, and the coordinator should confirm the rubric's basis. _(scope: station_area — Kamphaeng Phet MRT)_
- `kamphaeng-phet-c04` **[corroborated·tradeoff·outside]** Green 9 is a buffer effect, and the geometry says so: the app maps only 0.1 ha of park inside this unit, and the Overpass search found no major park centroid within 850 m of the station node — Chatuchak Park's centroid is ~1.4 km away, Queen Sirikit Park's ~1.1 km, Wachirabenchat's ~1.5 km (agent-computed straight-line from OSM centroids), with only two small dog parks and condominium gardens nearer. The big parks belong to the mo-chit/chatuchak units across the boundary; this unit's green score rides on their adjacency. _(scope: station_area — Chatuchak park complex edge)_
- `kamphaeng-phet-c05` **[corroborated·mixed·partial]** The unit's rhythm is the weekend market's: the blocks around Exits 1-2 host the Chatuchak Weekend Market — about 35 acres, 26-27 sections, with 200,000+ weekend visitors by the market site's count — while Or Tor Kor at Exit 3 trades daily with a morning peak and thins through the afternoon. Weekday Kamphaeng Phet is therefore a market-employee and produce-logistics day, in sharp contrast to the Saturday-Sunday visitor surge; no read source quantifies weekday footfall. _(scope: venue — Chatuchak Weekend Market / Or Tor Kor)_
- `kamphaeng-phet-c06` **[proposed·strength·inside]** Or Tor Kor (A.T.K.) market, at Exit 3, is run by the Marketing Organisation for Farmers and is described in the neighbourhood guide as 'the fresh market Bangkok recommends to itself' — graded and curated produce, seafood on ice, a large prepared-food section, covered and partly air-conditioned, expensive by Bangkok fresh-market standards. A widely repeated claim ranks it 4th-best fresh market in the world (CNN, 2017), but the CNN page now returns 404 and the rank could not be body-verified here — treat it as unverified heritage. _(scope: venue — Or Tor Kor Market)_
- `kamphaeng-phet-c07` **[conflicted·neutral·outside]** The long-distance bus edge of this unit's story is real but bounded: BEM lists the Mo Chit transport connection at Exit 1, while the actual Mo Chit 2 terminal (Bangkok Bus Terminal, Chatuchak — northern, Central and Isan routes, opened 8 April 1998) sits at 999 Kamphaeng Phet 2 Road behind Queen Sirikit Park, roughly 2 km from the Chatuchak-area rail stations per the terminal's article — outside this 800 m catchment. In 2025 the Transport Ministry was reported weighing consolidation of all three long-distance terminals at Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal. _(scope: other — Mo Chit 2 bus terminal)_
- `kamphaeng-phet-c08` **[proposed·strength·partial]** Rents cannot be confirmed at the doorstep but track the Chatuchak district blend: HipFlat's district averages (October 2026, 4,537 rental listings) run studio USD 446, one-bedroom USD 565, two-bedroom USD 984, with THB listings on the page at 16,000-25,000 baht for studios and small one-beds — consistent with the app's 16,500/34,000 Chatuchak blend at district level. None of the page's captured listings sat at Kamphaeng Phet MRT itself; the nearest transit references were the Lat Phrao corridor and a shuttle-van building serving MRT Chatuchak / BTS Mo Chit. _(scope: district — Chatuchak district)_
- `kamphaeng-phet-c09` **[proposed·neutral·entire_unit]** The station's ridership position underlines its specialist role: 1,164,679 passengers in 2021 against 6,865,636 at Chatuchak Park (BL13) a stop away — roughly one-sixth — meaning most through-traffic rides past rather than alighting here; the station lives or dies by its market, market-employee and bus-transfer functions. _(scope: station_area — Kamphaeng Phet MRT)_
- `kamphaeng-phet-c10` **[proposed·tradeoff·partial]** The market this station serves is mid-reorganisation: in February 2025 City Hall ordered 529 stallholders near the clock tower to vacate by 30 April 2025 for a redevelopment intended to create a new landmark, with only about 15 vendors accepting relocation offers and 122 in rent arrears — a change that lands directly on the exits this station is named for. _(scope: venue — Chatuchak Weekend Market)_

Weekend-market crowding is the one phenomenon with real numbers (200,000+/weekend). _(scope: station_area — Kamphaeng Phet unit)_
