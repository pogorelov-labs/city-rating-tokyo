## Card preview (proposed)
> Bangkok's river-rail hinge: one BTS platform on a bridge, a dozen boat lines below, and a riverside skyline of hotels, domes and one famous ruin.

## Guide lead (proposed)
Saphan Taksin is where the BTS network meets the Chao Phraya: a single-platform station on Taksin Bridge above Sathorn Pier, with Sathon, Bang Rak and a slice of Khlong San in its catchment — a transport-first address whose riverside glamour and residential quiet sit a few blocks apart.

## Strengths
- The boat interchange is unmatched: yellow, green-yellow and red express-boat lines terminate at Sathorn, with tourist boats, Thai Smile Boat, a 3.50-baht cross-river ferry and ICONSIAM shuttles alongside BTS. *[`saphan-taksin-c03`]*
- Riverside identity at street level — State Tower's rooftop cluster and the pier's hotel belt give the area a skyline-and-water rhythm few station areas have. *[`saphan-taksin-c05`, `saphan-taksin-c10`]*

## Trade-offs
- The single-platform, single-track layout structurally restricts Silom Line capacity and caps trains at 5 cars; the 2017 widening plan has been cancelled, leaving the bottleneck in place. *[`saphan-taksin-c02`, `saphan-taksin-c04`]*
- The green score rides on 5.2 ha no source can name, and the derelict Sathorn Unique tower stands about 200 m from the station with no recorded path to completion. *[`saphan-taksin-c08`, `saphan-taksin-c06`]*

## Claim index

- `saphan-taksin-c01` **[corroborated·neutral·entire_unit]** The unit anchors on BTS Silom Line station S6 'Saphan Taksin', opened 5 December 1999, built in the median of Taksin Bridge at Charoen Krung Road at the foot of the bridge; the Thai article places it across Bang Rak and Sathon districts, and the app catchment splits Sathon 47.1% / Bang Rak 29.4% / Khlong San 23.5% — the brief's 'Bang Rak blend' understates that Sathon is the app's primary district. _(scope: station_area — Saphan Taksin BTS station / Taksin Bridge)_
- `saphan-taksin-c02` **[corroborated·tradeoff·inside]** Saphan Taksin is the BTS network's one-of-a-kind single-platform station: only one platform and one track fit in the 16.5 m bridge median, both travel directions call at it via switches, the 115 m platform caps trains at 5 cars instead of 6, and the configuration 'restricts capacity on the entire line'; conceived as a temporary terminus, its closure was prevented by proximity to Sathorn Pier. _(scope: venue — Saphan Taksin BTS station)_
- `saphan-taksin-c03` **[corroborated·strength·inside]** Sathorn Pier beside the station is the river-network interchange: the official operator site (retrieved 2026-11) lists yellow, green-yellow and red-flag lines terminating at Sathorn on weekdays, while the orange line currently runs Nonthaburi–Wat Rajsingkorn on weekdays; the station was Bangkok's first rail stop with a boat interchange, and Thai Wikipedia adds the Chao Phraya Tourist Boat, Thai Smile Boat, and a 3.50-baht cross-river ferry (05:30-23:00) under Taksin Bridge, plus ICONSIAM shuttle boats. _(scope: venue — Sathorn (Central) Pier)_
- `saphan-taksin-c04` **[corroborated·tradeoff·inside]** The long-planned station capacity fix is stalled: a 2017 Transport Ministry resolution approved widening Taksin Bridge by 230 m and rebuilding platforms and tracks on both banks, but the project has since been cancelled, with BEM to hand completed designs to the BMA as the BTS concession runs to 2029; a 2017-reported BMA reconfiguration (1 billion baht, 40 months) never commenced. _(scope: venue — Saphan Taksin BTS station / Taksin Bridge)_
- `saphan-taksin-c05` **[corroborated·strength·partial]** State Tower (68 floors, 247 m, completed 2001, two lebua hotels and the 63rd-floor Sirocco restaurant) stands on Silom Road in Bang Rak adjacent Charoen Krung — roughly 330-400 m from the station by coordinates, so it very likely falls inside this unit (batch-2 also placed it inside bang-rak khet). _(scope: building — State Tower / lebua)_
- `saphan-taksin-c06` **[proposed·tradeoff·inside]** The 185 m, 49-floor Sathorn Unique 'ghost tower' — begun 1990 as a planned 600-unit luxury condominium and halted roughly 80-90% complete by the 1997 Asian financial crisis — stands officially 'on hold' about 200 m from the station by coordinates (off Charoen Krung Soi 51-53, Sathon district, close to Taksin Bridge), a derelict landmark and urban-exploration site with no recorded sale; access is officially off-limits. _(scope: building — Sathorn Unique Tower)_
- `saphan-taksin-c07` **[conflicted·neutral·partial]** Rent check against stated figures (24,000 / 52,500 THB, editorial blend): the accessible listing evidence is thin and conflicting — PropertyScout's Sathorn page exposes no aggregate, only a nearby 1-bed datapoint (Rhythm Charoenkrung Pavilion, 37 m², 24,500 THB), while unattributed search-summary aggregates give a Bang Rak median near 35,400 and 'typical' bands anywhere from 8-13k to 25-40k depending on platform. _(scope: district — Sathorn / Charoen Krung / Bang Rak rental corridors)_
- `saphan-taksin-c08` **[corroborated·tradeoff·entire_unit]** The green score (8) rests on an app figure of 5.2 ha of park that no accessible source maps to a named park: the closest named spaces — Saphan Taksin Park at BTS Exit 2 and the under-bridge garden on the Thonburi side — are consistently described as small, and no hectare figure was found for either. _(scope: station_area — Taksin Bridge riverfront green spaces)_
- `saphan-taksin-c09` **[proposed·neutral·entire_unit]** Rhythm: weekday commuter and boat-connection peaks (the pier is the network's main boat interchange) alternate with a steady tourist beat around the pier and riverside hotels; the single-platform configuration makes peak BTS queues a built-in feature of rush hours. _(scope: station_area — Saphan Taksin station / Sathorn pier nexus)_
