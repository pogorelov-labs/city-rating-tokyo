# v0.3.0 — Bangkok in 3D

Bangkok gains a tilted 3D map with buildings, rail stations, parks and water.
District ratings, station areas and the 200 m grid share the same scores,
filters and comparison controls. The quieter geographic palette gives the
rating colors priority; selected areas use a solid blue outline and compared
areas use a purple dashed outline.

At street zoom, station areas keep their names when you hide the rail overlay.
Tapping a rail stop outside the rated areas leaves your selection in place.
Choosing a district in search, rankings or a guide frames its boundary. Tapping
a district directly on the 3D map keeps the street view.

The map cards use the shared guide cards introduced in v0.2.0. Open a guide and
return to the map in the same tab to keep your filters, comparisons and weights.
A guide link opened in a new tab carries your weights. To share a selection
together with its filters and comparisons, use **Share** on the map.

Switching back to a city in the same tab restores that city's filters and
selections, even with custom weights. A city link opened in a fresh tab carries
only the weights. An invalid grid-cell reference is discarded without changing
the renderer or removing other valid settings from the link.

The standard map remains available in the map selector. It also provides a
fallback when 3D graphics or the initial map load are unavailable. Controls and
recovery messages support English, Japanese and Russian. Tokyo keeps its
existing map.

Map updates reuse geometry, labels and the grid texture. Weight changes avoid
unnecessary redraws, and the mobile detail card calculates one cell's score
instead of rescoring the whole city.

The default Bangkok grid filter now includes all eligible cells, and the family
filter agrees across the map, count and rankings. Shared links retain flood,
family and applicable Tokyo seismic settings. Existing shared links continue
to work.

This is a code release, not a rating-data refresh. Scoring rules, source dates
and existing rent/safety confidence limits remain unchanged. Building footprints
and heights depend on basemap coverage. The 3D basemap uses OpenFreeMap with
OpenMapTiles and OpenStreetMap attribution; no map API key is required.

Desktop measurements informed the implementation. Performance on physical
phones and slower graphics hardware varies; the standard map remains available.
